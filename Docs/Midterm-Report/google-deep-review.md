## Summary

This midterm report presents the design, implementation, and preliminary ablation results of a lightweight post-hoc convolutional head trained to predict per-cell depth failure probabilities ($\text{AbsRel} > 0.10$) for a frozen monocular depth foundation model (Depth Anything V2 ViT-B) across adverse driving conditions on the nuScenes dataset. The proposed architecture reads 64 frozen intermediate decoder features (`output_conv1`), mean-normalized disparity, ego-motion-compensated temporal spread across four neighboring frames, and sparse automotive radar residuals, training the head with positive-weighted binary cross-entropy against sparse LiDAR-derived error labels. Evaluating everything on a cached $132 \times 74$ grid across 149,410 images, the authors report sensor coverage statistics, ten completed ablation runs demonstrating that the frozen decoder features carry the vast majority of the error-predictive signal while raw radar channels currently tie the no-radar baseline, and a detailed milestone plan to complete sparsification benchmarks (AUSE/AURG) and downstream evaluation for the final report.

## Strengths

* **Practical Problem Formulation for Safety-Critical Perception:** The report tackles a highly relevant engineering problem in autonomous driving and robotics: modern monocular depth foundation models are frequently deployed as frozen, deterministic modules with zero dropout rates and no reliability measure, making a lightweight post-hoc confidence head a practical, low-latency alternative to deep ensembles or test-time backpropagation.
* **Rigorous Experimental Hygiene and Diagnostic Transparency:** The authors demonstrate commendable engineering and scientific rigor by caching frozen intermediate representations for reproducible single-factor ablations, thoroughly auditing sensor projection pitfalls (such as ego-trunk occlusion, parallax bleed-through, and missing radar elevation), and candidly reporting negative results where the noisy radar channel yields no global validation loss improvement.
* **Comprehensive Satisfaction of Course Guidelines:** The manuscript thoroughly addresses all four required components of the CSCI-566 midterm check-in (progress so far, literature review, initial results, and updated plan), supported by unit-tested evaluation code, automated model cards, and well-organized milestone tracking.

## Weaknesses

* **Privileged LiDAR Leakage in Inference Inputs and Oracle Scale Alignment:** While the introduction states that inference does not require LiDAR, the ego-motion-compensated temporal spread input channel (and a planned Stage 2 radar residual run) relies on a per-image least-squares scale-and-shift fit against keyframe LiDAR to lift 2D cells into metric 3D space, introducing privileged sensor leakage at test time. Furthermore, defining ground-truth error labels after per-image ordinary least-squares alignment absorbs global metric scale drift and allows localized outliers or adverse-weather LiDAR attenuation to skew the global regression line (Reference Paper: Towards Robust Monocular Depth Estimation: Mixing Datasets for Zero-Shot Cross-Dataset Transfer, René Ranftl, Katrin Lasinger, David Hafner, Konrad Schindler, and Vladlen Koltun, 2022; Reference Paper: Seeing Through Fog Without Seeing Fog: Deep Multimodal Sensor Fusion in Unseen Adverse Weather, Mario Bijelic, Tobias Gruber, Fahim Mannan, Florian Kraus, Werner Ritter, Klaus Dietmayer, and Felix Heide, 2020).
* **Mathematical Misalignment of Class-Weighted BCE Loss with Calibration (ECE) and Continuous Error Ranking (AUSE/AURG):** Weighting positive examples by $w_+ = 1.658$ in binary cross-entropy shifts the Bayes-optimal output logit by $+\ln(w_+)$, which systematically inflates predicted probabilities and degrades Expected Calibration Error (ECE) unless analytically recalibrated. Additionally, supervising with a hard binary cutoff ($\tau = 0.10$) provides no gradient incentive to order severe errors relative to one another, explaining why prolonged BCE training improved validation loss through Epoch 8 while degrading continuous ranking performance (AUSE) after Epoch 1 on the mini split (Reference Paper: On the Uncertainty of Self-Supervised Monocular Depth Estimation, Matteo Poggi, Filippo Aleotti, Fabio Tosi, and Stefano Mattoccia, 2020).
* **Omission of Closely Related Post-Hoc Supervision Literature and Key Baselines:** The literature review and baseline suite would be strengthened by situating the architecture within established supervised post-hoc failure prediction frameworks that attach auxiliary convolutional heads to frozen depth/perception decoders, alongside efficient multi-frame warping and learned radar-column association methods (Reference Paper: Addressing Failure Prediction by Learning Model Confidence, Charles Corbière, Nicolas Thome, Avner Bar-Hen, Matthieu Cord, and Patrick Pérez, 2019; Reference Paper: SLURP: Side Learning Uncertainty for Regression Problems, Xuanlong Yu, Gianni Franchi, and Emanuel Aldea, 2021; Reference Paper: Uncertainty from Motion for DNN Monocular Depth Estimation, Soumya Sudhakar, Vivienne Sze, and Sertac Karaman, 2022; Reference Paper: Depth Estimation from Camera Image and mmWave Radar Point Cloud, Akash Deep Singh, Yunhao Ba, Ankur Sarker, Howard Zhang, Achuta Kadambi, Stefano Soatto, Mani Srivastava, and Alex Wong, 2023). Moreover, the evaluation plan omits the gradient-based decoder uncertainty method highlighted as the nearest prior work in Section 2 as well as zero-parameter heuristic rankings using raw normalized disparity and raw radar residuals (Reference Paper: Gradient-Based Uncertainty for Monocular Depth Estimation, Julia Hornauer and Vasileios Belagiannis, 2022; Reference Paper: Depth Estimation from Monocular Images and Sparse Radar Data, Juan-Ting Lin, Dengxin Dai, and Luc Van Gool, 2020).
* **Dataset Partitioning Confounds, Incomplete Shard Camera Skew, and Evaluation Sequencing:** Coupling the random seed for the 10% dev scene split with the model initialization seed in `m1-seed1` confounds scene-difficulty variance with optimization variance, while the 5 incomplete cache shards create an uneven camera distribution (62.5% of `CAM_FRONT` vs. 80% of `CAM_FRONT_LEFT`) that interacts with camera-specific radar fit failure rates. Finally, please consider resolving open ground-truth label masks (ego-trunk occlusion), replacing naive pointwise confidence-depth multiplication in the downstream consumer plan with a mathematically principled formulation, and implementing scene-level block bootstrapping before running final validation benchmarks.

2. **Class-Weighted BCE Loss vs. Probabilistic Calibration (`ECE`) and Binarized Supervision ($\tau = 0.10$) vs. Continuous Sparsification Ranking (`AUSE` / `AURG`) (Section 3.1, pp. 3–4, lines 225–240; Section 3.3, p. 5, lines 314–315; Section 3.4, p. 5, lines 340–348; Section 4.2, p. 6, lines 446, 475–482; Table 3, p. 7, D14; Section 5.1, Item 2, p. 7)**
   * **Verified Implementation & Context:** In Section 3.1 (lines 237–240) and Section 4.2 (line 446), the confidence head is trained with Masked Binary Cross-Entropy (BCE) over labeled cells where positive examples ($\text{AbsRel} > 0.10$, comprising $\pi \approx 37.6\%$ of labeled training cells; Table 3, D12) are weighted by the negative-to-positive ratio $w_+ = \frac{1 - \pi}{\pi} \approx \frac{0.624}{0.376} = 1.658$. In Section 3.4 (lines 340–348), the evaluation package implements Expected Calibration Error (ECE) alongside rank-based sparsification metrics (`AUSE`, `AURG`, and `Rejection AUROC`) to score the predicted probabilities $p_{\text{error}} = \sigma(z)$, where $z$ is the output logit and $\sigma(\cdot)$ is the logistic sigmoid. Additionally, Section 4.2 (lines 475–482) and Table 3 (Deviation D14) highlight a critical empirical finding on `nuScenes v1.0-mini`: while dev Masked BCE loss decreased monotonically and was lowest after epoch 8, the primary evaluation metric—dev AUSE—was lowest (best) after epoch 1 and degraded in subsequent epochs. Under D14, the pipeline selects `best.pt` using lowest dev BCE loss (which selects epoch 8 in every run in Table 5 and Figure 3) and schedules a longer 16-epoch run in Stage 4.
   * **Mathematical & Methodological Analysis:**
     1. *Definitive Mathematical Distortion of ECE under Class-Weighted BCE:* Weighting positive examples by $w_+ > 1$ in BCE alters the Bayes-optimal scoring function. For a grid cell with input features $\mathbf{x}$ and true conditional error probability $p^*(\mathbf{x}) = P(Y = 1 \mid \mathbf{x})$, the conditional expected pointwise weighted BCE risk for logit $z$ is:
        $$\mathcal{L}(z; \mathbf{x}) = -w_+ p^*(\mathbf{x}) \ln \sigma(z) - (1 - p^*(\mathbf{x})) \ln(1 - \sigma(z))$$
        Setting $\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial z} = 0$ yields the Bayes-optimal weighted logit $z^*(\mathbf{x})$:
        $$\frac{\sigma(z^*(\mathbf{x}))}{1 - \sigma(z^*(\mathbf{x}))} = w_+ \frac{p^*(\mathbf{x})}{1 - p^*(\mathbf{x})} \implies z^*(\mathbf{x}) = \ln \frac{p^*(\mathbf{x})}{1 - p^*(\mathbf{x})} + \ln w_+$$
        Passing $z^*(\mathbf{x})$ directly through a standard sigmoid yields:
        $$\hat{p}_{\text{weighted}}(\mathbf{x}) = \sigma(z^*(\mathbf{x})) = \frac{w_+ p^*(\mathbf{x})}{w_+ p^*(\mathbf{x}) + (1 - p^*(\mathbf{x}))}$$
        Consequently, a well-converged network trained with $w_+ = 1.658$ outputs logits shifted upward by $\ln(1.658) \approx +0.5056$. For a cell whose true empirical error probability equals the dataset base rate ($p^*(\mathbf{x}) = 0.376$), the network's raw predicted probability converges to $0.50$ rather than $0.376$ (an upward bias of $\approx 12.4$ percentage points). While rank-based metrics (`AUSE`, `AURG`, `Rejection AUROC`, and relative image ordering in the Frame Selector) are strictly invariant to monotonically increasing transformations of the logits, **ECE is not invariant**. Evaluating ECE directly on raw $\sigma(z)$ from a class-weighted BCE model artificially penalizes the head for systematic over-prediction of error probabilities induced purely by the loss weighting.
     2. *Hard Binary Thresholding vs. Continuous Error Ranking and Late-Epoch Logit Saturation (Potential Concern in D14):* Under a hard binary step target $\mathbf{1}[\text{AbsRel} > \tau]$ at $\tau = 0.10$, the BCE loss assigns the exact same positive label ($Y = 1$) to a mild borderline error ($\text{AbsRel} = 0.11$) and a catastrophic outlier ($\text{AbsRel} = 2.50$), providing zero gradient signal to order them relative to one another; likewise, errors of $0.09$ and $0.01$ both receive $Y = 0$. Conversely, on an `AUSE` / `AURG` sparsification curve evaluating continuous $\text{AbsRel}$, removing a cell with $\text{AbsRel} = 2.50$ in the first $1\%$ of sparsification reduces the remaining mean error roughly $25\times$ more than removing a cell with $\text{AbsRel} = 0.11$. After epoch 1, network logits remain smooth and correlate broadly with visual feature difficulty and error magnitude (yielding low AUSE). As training progresses toward epoch 8—especially as the cosine annealing schedule decays the learning rate to near zero at the preset horizon $T_{\max} = 8$—minimizing BCE drives logits toward extreme saturation ($\pm\infty$) to sharpen the step-function boundary specifically around $\tau = 0.10$ and overfit noisy boundary cells. Once cells with $\text{AbsRel} = 0.15$ and $\text{AbsRel} = 0.80$ both saturate near $p_{\text{error}} \approx 1.0$, minor boundary adjustments can scramble their relative ordering, worsening the early part of the sparsification curve (inflating AUSE) even as BCE continues to drop.
   * **Actionable Recommendations:**
     * *Zero-Cost Analytical Recalibration for ECE:* No retraining is required. In `confhead/src/evals` (Item 2), when computing calibrated probabilities for ECE (and for downstream confidence weighting), apply the exact analytical Bayes prior-shift correction to the logits before the sigmoid:
       $$\hat{p}_{\text{calibrated}}(\mathbf{x}) = \sigma\!\left(z(\mathbf{x}) - \ln w_+\right) = \sigma\!\left(z(\mathbf{x}) - \ln(1.658)\right)$$
       Reporting ECE both before and after subtracting $\ln w_+$ (or alongside single-parameter post-hoc Temperature/Platt scaling fitted on the dev split) requires only one line of code in the scoring script and demonstrates rigorous command of probabilistic calibration theory.

3. **Spatial Discretization: Resolution Mismatch, Feature Area-Averaging vs. "Nearest-Point-Wins" Boundary Aliasing, and Head Receptive Field (Title; Section 1, lines 002, 031, 045–050; Section 3.1, p. 3, lines 219–237; Table 1, p. 3; Figure 2, p. 4; Table 3, p. 7, D3, D6–D9)**
   * **Verified Implementation & Context:** While the title and Section 1 motivate *"per-pixel confidence"* (lines 002, 031, 080), the network predicts the *error probability* $p_{\text{error}} = P(\text{AbsRel} > \tau)$ (where higher values indicate *lower* confidence) on a coarse $132 \times 74$ grid where each cell spans roughly $12 \times 12 \approx 150$ pixels in the $900 \times 1600$ camera image (or $7 \times 7$ pixels in the $924 \times 518$ ViT-B input). Decoder features (`output_conv1`) are downsampled to this grid via **area-averaging** (Table 1, Ch. 0–63), whereas ground-truth labels are assigned via a **winner-take-all rule**: *"the nearest point wins a cell"* (Section 3.1, lines 227–228; Table 3, D7). With $\approx 3,600$ LiDAR returns mapping onto $\approx 2,450$ labeled cells per image (lines 230–232), multi-point collisions occur in roughly a third of labeled cells ($\approx 1.47$ points per labeled cell on average, and higher along dense horizontal LiDAR scan lines).
   * **Methodological Analysis (Potential Concerns):**
     1. *Feature-Label Boundary Aliasing at Depth Discontinuities:* In driving scenes, a $12 \times 12$ pixel block at medium-to-long range frequently straddles depth discontinuities (e.g., a foreground vehicle edge, pedestrian, or pole at $z = 6\text{ m}$ against a background building/sky at $z = 45\text{ m}$). Because monocular depth networks concentrate their largest relative smoothing errors along occlusion boundaries, area-averaging decoder features over a $12 \times 12$ block while assigning the cell's ground-truth binary label solely from the minimum-depth foreground LiDAR point ($z = 6\text{ m}$) creates a spatial aliasing asymmetry: even if the foreground LiDAR return only grazes the corner of the $12 \times 12$ cell while $90\%$ of the cell's area is background at $45\text{ m}$, the input feature blends foreground and background activations while the target reflects only the $6\text{ m}$ return. Conversely, if a near point slightly inside the foreground object has low error ($\text{AbsRel} \le 0.10$) while another return in the same cell right on the boundary edge has high error ($\text{AbsRel} > 0.10$), nearest-point selection masks the high-error return and labels the cell reliable ($Y = 0$). Furthermore, where occlusion bleed-through occurs (Table 3, D9: background LiDAR points projecting through a nearer object due to LiDAR-camera baseline parallax), if no foreground LiDAR return lands in that specific $12 \times 12$ cell, the background bleed-through point wins the cell and labels a foreground visual feature with background depth error.
     2. *Head Receptive Field and Unclipped Radar Standardization:* The confidence head consists of three $3 \times 3$ convolutions (widths 64, 64, 32) with ReLU followed by a $1 \times 1$ convolution (Section 3.1, lines 235–237), yielding an effective receptive field of $7 \times 7$ grid cells ($\approx 85 \times 85$ raw pixels). While compact and fast to train, two architectural details interact with sparse radar (`Ch. 66–67`): (a) because radar validity (at radius 1) covers only $3.4\%$ of grid cells, any labeled cell more than 3 grid cells away from a valid radar cell lies completely outside the $7 \times 7$ receptive field of any radar measurement; and (b) when `Radar residual` (Channel 66) is unclipped (Table 3, D6) and set to $0$ wherever radar is absent or the radar fit fails, $0.0$ in Channel 66 ambiguously encodes both *perfect radar agreement* ($|\hat{d}-z|/z = 0$) and *missing radar*, while unclipped relative error spikes ($\gg 10$) from elevation-free radar returns projecting onto distant sky/background inflate the training-set standard deviation $\sigma_{66}$ during global Z-score standardization (lines 221–223), compressing normal radar residuals ($0.05\text{--}0.50$) into a narrow numerical band.
   * **Actionable Recommendations:**
     * Explicitly clarify in Section 1 that confidence is defined as $1 - p_{\text{error}}$ on a $132 \times 74$ superpixel/patch grid ($1/12\times$ raw image resolution), and clarify in Section 3.1 whether the pixel-level prediction $\hat{d}$ in $\text{AbsRel}$ is sampled at the exact sub-pixel LiDAR projection before cell assignment (as indicated by Section 3.2, lines 273–274: *"the lidar and radar points with the model disparity at each"*) or taken from the cell-averaged disparity grid.
     * Because cached `.npz` entries store raw projected LiDAR/radar points and compute cell labels and radar channels on-the-fly at load time (Section 3.2, lines 273–277), inspect or report as a diagnostic statistic how often points within the same cell disagree on $\text{AbsRel} > 0.10$ and how much the positive label rate ($37.6\%$) shifts under **mean/median cell $\text{AbsRel}$** (matching area-averaged features) or **max-error pooling** ($\max_{i \in \text{cell}} \text{AbsRel}_i > \tau$, worst-case safety labeling). In Stage 2 (`m2-*`), consider clipping Channel 66 to $[0, 1]$ or $[0, 2]$ (as originally planned in D6) prior to Z-score standardization, and point out the $7 \times 7$ cell receptive field limitation when discussing why sparse radar requires a wider splatting radius or dilated convolutions to propagate spatially.

4. **Incomplete Shard Sampling Bias Across Camera Viewpoints and Dev Split Capping (Section 3.1, p. 4, lines 244–245; Section 3.2, p. 4, lines 254–270; Table 3, p. 7, D10; Section 5.4, p. 10, lines 629–636)**
   * **Verified Implementation & Context:** The trainval cache was built as a 16-shard GPU job array where each shard processes every 16th image (`index % 16 == shard_id`). Because 11 of the 16 shards completed while 5 stopped early at $\approx 13\%$ completion, the cache holds $73\%$ of trainval ($149,410 / 204,894$ images: $123,079$ train and $26,331$ of $36,114$ val), and as the authors astutely diagnosed, camera views are sampled unevenly: $62.5\%$ of `CAM_FRONT` vs. $80\%$ of `CAM_FRONT_LEFT` (lines 260–267). Additionally, the dev split takes $10\%$ of training scenes and caps them at $3,000$ images (Section 3.1, lines 244–245).
   * **Methodological Analysis (Potential Concerns):**
     1. *Modulo-16 Interaction with 6-Camera Multi-View Indexing:* Why did `index % 16` cause camera coverage to range from $62.5\%$ to $80\%$? In nuScenes, sample records iterate sequentially across the **6 cameras**. Because $\gcd(6, 16) = 2$, a stride of 16 shifts the camera index by $16 \equiv 4 \pmod 6$ at each step, cycling with period $\operatorname{lcm}(6, 16) = 48$. Consequently, individual shards sample only 3 of the 6 cameras (exclusively even-indexed cameras $\{0, 2, 4\}$ or odd-indexed cameras $\{1, 3, 5\}$) within uniform blocks, so early termination of 5 shards systematically under-samples specific camera viewpoints. Because `CAM_FRONT` has a narrower horizontal field of view ($70^\circ$), the longest longitudinal viewing range, and denser front radar returns, whereas back-side cameras have much higher radar fit failure rates ($32\%$ vs. $17.0\%$ overall) and `CAM_BACK` has ego-trunk occlusion (D8), evaluating the validation split on the incomplete $73\%$ cache biases aggregate validation metrics toward side-camera characteristics and prevents exact sample-count parity with the published BEVCar validation splits ($4,449$ day, $968$ rain, and $602$ night keyframes).
     2. *Dev Split Capping Strategy:* Section 3.1 (lines 244–245) does not specify whether the $3,000$-image cap on the dev split is drawn via uniform/stratified sampling across all selected dev scenes and 6 cameras or by taking the first $3,000$ images sequentially. Sequential truncation evaluates only the first $\approx 17\text{--}20$ scenes rather than all selected dev scenes, distorting day/rain/night proportions and substantially increasing scene-selection variance across random seeds.
   * **Actionable Recommendations:**
     * Because resubmitting the cache build job automatically skips existing entries and fills only the missing $27\%$ gap (lines 268–270), prioritize finishing the 5 incomplete shards (or at minimum the missing validation entries so all 150 validation scenes have $100\%$ balanced 6-camera coverage across all $36,114$ validation images, while optionally keeping the training split fixed to the existing $123,079$-image index for parity with Stages 0–1) before running final validation scoring. Completing the validation cache requires no retraining of the 10 existing heads. If cluster queue constraints force scoring on the $73\%$ validation cache, report **per-camera validation metrics** alongside a **camera-balanced macro-average** so viewpoint sampling skew does not distort the overall conclusions.
     * Clarify in Section 3.1 how the $3,000$ dev images are sampled across the selected dev scenes.

5. **Evaluation Protocol Specifications: Per-Image Macro vs. Pooled Micro Aggregation, Single-Class AUROC Edge Cases, and Scene-Level Block Bootstrapping (Section 3.4, p. 5, lines 337–372; Table 3, p. 7, D13; Section 5.1, Items 2 & 5, pp. 7–8)**
   * **Verified Implementation & Context:** The team has built a well-structured, unit-tested evaluation package (`confhead/src/evals` with 79 passing tests, including synthetic checks for perfect, random, reversed, and constant sparsification curves) and reproducible checkpoint tracking on Hugging Face. Section 3.4 and Section 5.1 (Items 2 and 5) identify two open methodological items still to be finalized: (1) *"the AUROC of an image where every cell is correct or every cell is wrong"* (lines 348–349, 510–511), and (2) *"the bootstrap confidence intervals, are still to be written"* to show which differences across the BEVCar day (111 scenes), rain (24 scenes), and night (15 scenes) groups are real (lines 369–370, 534–536).
   * **Methodological & Statistical Guidance (Potential Concerns):**
     1. *Macro (Per-Image) vs. Micro (Dataset-Pooled) Metric Aggregation and Single-Class AUROC:* In binary classification, AUROC is mathematically undefined when all targets in a sample belong to a single class ($\sum_i Y_i = 0$ or $\sum_i Y_i = N$), because either the True Positive Rate ($\text{TP}/P$) or False Positive Rate ($\text{FP}/N$) has a zero denominator ($0/0$). Assigning an arbitrary fallback value (such as $0.5$ or $1.0$) to homogeneous frames introduces statistical bias into per-image macro-averages because easy daytime scenes have more all-correct frames than nighttime scenes. Furthermore, while `AUSE` and `AURG` in monocular depth uncertainty (Poggi et al., 2020) must be computed **per image and macro-averaged**—because per-image least-squares scale/shift fits make error scales frame-specific, so pooling cells globally across frames would conflate *within-image pixel ranking* with *cross-image baseline difficulty*—calibration (`ECE`) and global threshold discrimination (`Rejection AUROC`) are naturally well-defined when **micro-averaged (pooled) across all labeled cells** in a condition split.
     2. *Temporal and Multi-Camera Autocorrelation (Pseudoreplication) in Bootstrap Confidence Intervals:* The $26,331$ (up to $36,114$) validation images come from $150$ continuous 20-second driving scenes ($111$ day, $24$ rain, $15$ night; lines 351–358). Consecutive keyframes (2 Hz across 6 synchronized cameras, $\approx 175\text{--}240$ images per scene) share highly correlated lighting, precipitation, road geometry, and traffic participants. If bootstrap confidence intervals are computed by resampling individual grid cells ($N \approx 6.5 \times 10^7$) or individual camera images ($N = 26,331$) independently with replacement, the statistical independence assumption is violated (pseudoreplication) and the resulting $95\%$ confidence intervals will be artificially narrow by roughly $\sqrt{175} \approx 13\times$, inflating false-positive significance when comparing models.
   * **Actionable Recommendations:**
     * **AUSE / AURG:** Compute sparsification curves **per image** over its valid LiDAR-labeled cells and macro-average across valid validation images, explicitly stating whether each image's sparsification curve is normalized by its initial unsparsified error or integrated in raw $\text{AbsRel}$ units.
     * **Rejection AUROC, ECE & No-Fit Frames (D13):** Report **dataset-pooled (micro-averaged) Rejection AUROC** across all labeled cells in each split/condition (which never encounters the single-class $0/0$ division and aligns directly with pooled ECE), and if you also report **per-image macro-averaged AUROC**, explicitly exclude single-class frames ($\sum_i Y_i \in \{0, N_i\}$) and zero-label frames where the LiDAR fit failed (Deviation D13) from the mean while logging the exact count of excluded frames per condition.
     * **Paired Scene-Level Block Bootstrapping:** Implement **scene-level cluster (block) bootstrapping** for Item 5 by pre-computing per-scene metric summaries across the $150$ validation scenes once, and resampling the **scenes** with replacement ($B = 1,000$ iterations over the $111$ day, $24$ rain, and $15$ night scenes, keeping all images within a sampled scene together). Furthermore, because every model is evaluated on the exact same validation scenes, compute bootstrap confidence intervals on the **paired per-scene differences** ($\Delta\text{AUSE} = \text{AUSE}_{\text{model A}} - \text{AUSE}_{\text{model B}}$ and $\Delta\text{AURG}$), which removes scene-to-scene difficulty variance, provides substantially higher statistical power for ablations, executes in milliseconds in NumPy, and respects temporal and multi-camera correlation.

1. **Confounded Experimental Factor in Seed Ablation (`m1-seed1`) Mixing Model Initialization Variance with Scene-Split Variance (Section 4.2, p. 6; Table 5 & Figure 3, p. 8):**
   * **Observation:** In Section 4.2 (lines 471–474) and Table 5, the run `m1-seed1` is designed to measure the pipeline's sensitivity to random initialization and batch ordering (answering the Stage 1 question: *"how big is the seed noise?"*). However, a single global random seed currently governs both the neural network training stochasticity (weight initialization and `DataLoader` shuffling) and the random sampling of the 10% development scene split (69 dev scenes out of 700 training scenes). Consequently, changing the seed in `m1-seed1` re-sampled the dev split, yielding a dev loss of $0.5885$ compared to $0.5566$ for the baseline `run-v1-radar` ($\Delta = +0.0319$—nearly $80\times$ larger than the same-split repeat difference of $0.0004$) and forcing the exclusion of `m1-seed1` from the dev-set comparisons in Section 4.2 and Figure 3c.
   * **Methodological Guidance:** While the authors appropriately recognize this incompatibility and defer `m1-seed1`'s comparison to the fixed `val` split, coupling dataset-partitioning randomness with model-optimization randomness is a substantive potential concern for experimental tracking and the upcoming Stage 4 and Stage 5 multi-seed runs (`m5-*`):
     1. *Confounded Validation & Checkpoint Selection:* In autonomous driving datasets like nuScenes, scene difficulty varies substantially across weather, lighting, and traffic density (as shown in Table 4, where nighttime median error is $0.079$ vs. $0.052$ by day). When the dev split shifts with the model seed, validation scene-sampling variance completely masks optimization variance, and checkpoint selection (`best.pt` via Deviation D14) operates on inconsistent validation distributions across seeds.
     2. *Shifted Training Distribution:* Because the 90% training split (631 scenes) is the complement of the 10% dev split (69 scenes), changing the split seed means `m1-seed1` was also trained on a partially different set of driving scenes than the other nine runs, confounding training-distribution variance with initialization variance even when evaluated on the fixed `val` set.
   * **Actionable Recommendation:** Strictly decouple the data-partitioning random seed from the model-training random seed—or commit a static list of the 631 training and 69 dev scene IDs to a fixed configuration file (just as was done for the BEVCar validation scene lists). Holding the train/dev scene split fixed while varying only the PyTorch initialization and shuffling seed will allow `m1-seed1` and Stage 5 runs (`m5-*`) to isolate true optimization stochasticity. Furthermore, even without retraining `m1-seed1`, you can evaluate its saved checkpoint on the baseline's 69 dev scenes (paying mind that some of those 69 scenes were in `m1-seed1`'s training split) or compare it purely on the held-out `val` split alongside fixed-split Stage 5 runs.

2. **Radar Geometry, Elevation Ambiguity, Least-Squares Fit Breakdown, and Supervision Overlap Dilution (Section 4.1 & 4.2, p. 6; Table 3 D6 & Table 4, p. 7; Table 5, p. 8):**
   * **Observation:** Section 4.1 (lines 415–423, 430–440) and Table 3 (D6) provide a thorough diagnostic of why the radar channels (Ch. 66–67) tie the no-radar baseline (`run-v0-no-radar` at $0.5562$ vs. `run-v1-radar` at $0.5566$, $\Delta = -0.0004$):
     1. Across 5 accumulated sweeps with a 1-cell radius, valid radar points cover only $3.4\%$ of grid cells and overlap with only $4.8\%$ of lidar-labeled cells.
     2. Per-image ordinary least-squares (OLS) fitting of disparity to radar fails completely on $17.0\%$ of trainval images ($32\%$ on the two back-side cameras), resulting in all-zero radar channels on those frames.
     3. Where the fit succeeds, the median radar residual is $0.25$ under the radar fit and $0.28$ under the lidar fit—roughly $5\times$ the lidar-measured depth error—because nuScenes radars lack vertical elevation resolution.
   * **3D Perception Guidance & Potential Concerns:** This diagnostic analysis is a strong aspect of the report. Two fundamental mechanisms in 3D perception explain why this setup prevents radar from moving global metrics and suggest concrete improvements for Stage 2 (`m2-*`) and the final evaluation:
     1. *Global Loss and Metric Dilution:* Because Masked BCE (and full-image AUSE) averages unweighted over all $\sim 2,450$ lidar-labeled cells per image—and $95.2\%$ of those labeled cells have `radar_validity = 0`—any gradient or loss reduction produced by radar on the $4.8\%$ overlap region is diluted by a factor of $\sim 20\times$ in the global average. Even a substantial local improvement on radar-valid cells would shift global dev loss by only $\sim 0.0002\text{–}0.0005$, falling entirely inside the $\pm 0.001$ tie band. Consequently, a tie in global dev loss does not by itself prove whether the head uses radar where radar returns actually exist.
     2. *Elevation Ambiguity ($z=0$ Projection) and OLS Fragility:* Continental ARS 408-21 radars on nuScenes record returns strictly in the 2D horizontal sensor plane ($z = 0$). When a 3D radar return from an elevated reflector (e.g., a vehicle roof, sign, or bridge) or uneven road is projected into the 2D camera perspective grid assuming zero elevation, it lands on a fixed horizon line that frequently corresponds to background sky, distant buildings, or the road surface rather than the reflecting object. Fitting an unregularized 2-parameter $L_2$ OLS line to sparse radar points ($\sim 150$ points across 5 sweeps, and $\sim 25$ per camera) in the presence of these elevation mismatches and multipath reflections makes the least-squares slope highly vulnerable to leverage outliers, causing degenerate/negative slopes ($17\%\text{–}32\%$ fit failure rate) and the inflated $0.25$ residual.
   * **Actionable Recommendation:**
     1. *Stratified Conditional Evaluation:* In the validation scoring script, compute AUSE, AURG, AUROC, and BCE separately on the **subset of lidar-labeled cells where `radar_validity == 1`** (comparing `run-v1-radar` directly against `run-v0-no-radar` on those exact cells). This requires no retraining or cache rebuilding and cleanly isolates whether the confidence head extracts useful signal from radar at the locations where radar measurements are present.
     2. *Robust Fitting and Vertical Pillar Projection:* To stabilize a strictly lidar-free radar fit at load time, replace naive OLS with a robust estimator such as **RANSAC** or **Huber / $L_1$ regression** (coupled with a non-negativity constraint on scale). Furthermore, in the radar-camera fusion literature (e.g., Lin et al., 2020; Singh et al., 2023; Schramm et al., 2024), missing elevation is resolved not only by isotropic 2D circle dilation (`radius 1` or `2`), but by expanding each 3D radar point into a **vertical 3D pillar** (e.g., spanning height $z \in [0.2\text{ m}, 2.0\text{ m}]$ above the ground plane), projecting the pillar into a vertical column of camera grid cells, and taking the minimum disparity residual along that column. Because raw radar points are already stored in each cached `.npz` entry, robust regression or vertical column matching can be tested at load time without re-running the backbone.

3. **Statistical Reliability of the Single-Pair Tie Band ($\pm 0.001$) and Architectural Channel Imbalance (Section 4.2, p. 6; Table 5, p. 8):**
   * **Observation:** In Section 4.2 (lines 453–458), the authors compare two identical training runs (`run1` at $0.5562$ and `run-v1-radar` at $0.5566$), observe a difference of $0.0004$, and establish a tie band of $\pm 0.001$ to adjudicate input ablations—concluding that removing the depth channel (`m1-no-depth`, $\Delta = +0.0008$) and removing radar (`run-v0-no-radar`, $\Delta = -0.0004$) are ties with the baseline, while the 64 decoder features carry almost all the signal (`m1-no-features`, $\Delta = +0.0601$).
   * **Methodological Guidance:**
     1. *Single-Degree-of-Freedom Variance Estimation:* Estimating an experimental noise floor from $n = 2$ runs yields only $1$ degree of freedom (sample standard deviation $s \approx 0.00028$). Under a Student's $t$-distribution with $\text{df} = 1$, a 95% confidence interval spans $\pm 12.71 \times \text{SE}$ ($\approx \pm 0.0025$), meaning two stochastic runs can easily land within $0.0004$ by chance even if the true multi-run standard deviation is several times larger.
     2. *Dimensionality Imbalance ($64 : 1$) and Semantic Context Bottleneck:* In the confidence head architecture (`68 -> 64 -> 64 -> 32 -> 1` via $3\times 3$ convolutions with weight decay $10^{-4}$), 64 standardized channels come from `output_conv1`, whereas normalized depth is 1 channel, temporal spread is 1 channel, and radar is 2 channels. Concatenating 64 correlated semantic/geometric feature maps with single scalar channels at the first convolution naturally biases gradient updates toward the 64-channel representation. Moreover, when training single-channel heads (`m1-depth-only` at $0.6394$ and `m1-temporal-only` at $0.6778$), feeding a single scalar map into a 3-layer $3\times 3$ CNN (receptive field of $7\times 7$ grid cells $\approx 84\times 84$ raw pixels) provides only local spatial gradients of disparity or temporal variance without any visual/semantic context (e.g., whether a region is sky, reflective glass, dark shadow, or road surface), explaining why scalar channels perform poorly in isolation.
   * **Actionable Recommendation:** Treat $\pm 0.001$ on aggregate dev BCE loss as a preliminary sanity check rather than a formal statistical cutoff. When building the validation comparison tables, rely on the paired scene-level bootstrap confidence intervals across the 150 validation scenes combined with the multi-seed runs in Stage 5.

4. **3D-to-2D Lidar Label Hygiene: Impact of Ego-Trunk Returns (D8) on Per-Image Least-Squares Fitting and Mitigation of Parallax Bleed-Through (D9) via Grid Min-Pooling (Section 4.1, p. 6; Table 3 D7–D9, p. 7; Section 5.1, p. 8):**
   * **Observation:** Section 4.1 (lines 424–429) and Table 3 (D8, D9) quantify two multi-sensor projection pitfalls on `v1.0-mini`: $3.2\%$ of `CAM_BACK` lidar pixels fall on the ego vehicle's own trunk (D8), and $0.35\%$ of lidar pixels are background returns that bleed through occluded foreground objects due to translational parallax between the roof-mounted 32-beam lidar and the cameras (D9). Both remain unmasked (`Open` in Table 3) in the 10 trained runs and are scheduled for resolution in Section 5.1 (Item 7, p. 8).

#### Potential Mistakes and Improvements

1. **Mathematical Formulation of Downstream Confidence Weighting (Section 5.1, Item 11, p. 9):**
   * **Potential Concern:** Item 11 states: *"Use of the confidence. Weight the depth by the confidence and report one number that improves; the team chooses the number."* If "weighting the depth by the confidence" is implemented literally as a pointwise multiplication—such as $\hat{d}_{\text{weighted}}(u, v) = (1 - p_{\text{error}}(u, v)) \cdot \hat{d}(u, v)$—this operation is mathematically ill-posed for continuous depth regression. Multiplying a range estimate by a probability scalar in $[0, 1]$ does not correct depth errors; instead, it systematically shrinks predicted depths toward zero (pulling uncertain distant background objects closer to the ego vehicle) whenever the model is uncertain, which will increase rather than decrease standard metric depth errors like AbsRel or RMSE.
   * **Suggested Improvement:** To rigorously demonstrate the downstream utility of the predicted confidence $c_i = 1 - p_{\text{error}, i}$ without retraining a large 3D perception network, replace direct pointwise depth multiplication with one or more of the following mathematically principled formulations:
     1. *Confidence-Weighted Least-Squares (CWLS) Scale-and-Shift Alignment:* In a deployed camera-radar system without LiDAR, the affine scale and shift parameters $(s, t)$ mapping relative disparity $\text{disp}_i$ to inverse depth must be fitted against sparse, noisy radar returns $z_{\text{radar}, i}^{-1}$. Weighting each sensor residual in the least-squares objective by the head's predicted confidence $w_i = 1 - p_{\text{error}, i}$,
        $$\min_{s, t} \sum_{i \in \mathcal{R}} w_i \left( s \cdot \text{disp}_i + t - z_{\text{radar}, i}^{-1} \right)^2,$$
        down-weights cells where the model predicts high error or multipath disagreement. You can then evaluate whether the resulting aligned depth map achieves a lower whole-image AbsRel against ground-truth LiDAR compared to an unweighted radar fit.
     2. *Confidence-Weighted Temporal Depth Fusion:* Because the cache pipeline already computes ego-motion-compensated inverse depth across neighboring frames ($\pm 1, \pm 2$ at 12 Hz), compute a confidence-weighted average of the multi-frame depth predictions at cell $i$, $\hat{d}_i^{\text{fused}} = \frac{\sum_t c_{i,t} \hat{d}_{i,t}}{\sum_t c_{i,t}}$ (analogous to inverse-variance weighting in Kalman filtering), and verify whether the fused depth achieves lower AbsRel than single-frame depth or an unweighted temporal mean.
